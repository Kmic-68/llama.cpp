fix: an uncompressed peer copy could overwrite the all-reduce buffer before its reader ran

The tensor-parallel all-reduce (ggml-backend-meta.cpp, allreduce_fallback) lands every exchange in
the same per-device reduction buffer, bcj.bufs[i_buf], and folds it into the partial with an ADD
queued on the destination's compute stream. Since the peer copies moved to a dedicated copy stream
(27961ce6c, for full-duplex PCIe), a copy is ordered against the source only: it waits on the
source's work marker. A source that had raced ahead to the next layer could therefore overwrite the
destination's reduction buffer before the destination's ADD of the previous layer had read it --
that ADD then summed the wrong partial. The compressed (f16) path already guarded exactly this
reuse with peer_stage_free; the uncompressed path had no equivalent.

Uncompressed exchanges are every decode step, every MTP draft/verify batch, every prompt tail below
512 tokens, and every exchange of a configuration whose partials are not f16-exact.

Found as sporadic whole-chunk corruption with fp32 matmuls (GGML_CUDA_CUBLAS_COMPUTE_TYPE=f32, no
P2P): 3 of 10 unhooked 15-chunk perplexity runs diverged -- two into NaN, one silently, every chunk
from the third on off by ~2e-5 nats. Per-node hashing could not see it: any host synchronize
between graph computes orders the destination's ADD first. Proven instead with a test build that
delays the destination's ADD with dummy sgemms on its own stream. That makes the race certain, and
it corrupts the *production* decode path -- MTP at 64 tokens greedy, fp16, P2P: different text and
43.4% draft acceptance against 78.8%. With the fix, delayed or not, the text and the acceptance
rate are identical to the undelayed run, and 6 of 6 unhooked fp32 runs agree chunk for chunk.

Fix: before an uncompressed copy, the copy stream also waits on the destination's work marker,
which graph_compute records after every graph -- including the meta backend's ADD, which is always
enqueued before the next layer's copies are issued. Waiting on the marker as it stands keeps the
two directions overlapped; re-recording it at copy time would also capture this exchange's wait on
the opposite copy and serialise them again -- measured at 2.9% of tg256 and 3.3% of MTP.

Cost of the kept form, production flags, interleaved and cooled: tg256 31.04 -> 30.81 (-0.7%),
MTP 55.31 -> 54.98 (-0.6%).
